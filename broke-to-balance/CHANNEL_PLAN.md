## 1. Monetization: what YouTube actually requires (verify in YouTube Studio → Earn; rules change)
| Tier | Subscribers | Watch time / Shorts views | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early (fan funding) | 500 | 3,000 public watch hours in 12 mo **or** 3M public Shorts views in 90 days, plus 3 public uploads in 90 days | memberships, Super Thanks, shopping |
| Full YPP (ads) | 1,000 | 4,000 public watch hours in 12 mo **or** 10M public Shorts views in 90 days | ad revenue share |

Also required: 2-step verification, no active strikes, an AdSense account, and a pass of YouTube's review of **originality**.

### Risks to plan around (important)
1. **"Inauthentic / mass-produced content" policy.** YouTube can refuse monetization to channels whose videos look templated, repetitive or mass-produced (same visuals + synthetic voice, only the topic changes). One video a day with an AI voice and the same layout is the profile this targets. **Mitigations:** your own voice (planned), a different visual hook/format per episode, original examples and spreadsheets, a real point of view in every script.
2. **Finance = sensitive category.** Keep "educational, not advice" disclaimers, label hypotheticals, no guaranteed-return claims, no personalized advice, no income promises. Advertisers are cautious here.
3. **Cadence vs. quality.** The pipeline can render a video in ~10 minutes, but scripts, facts and your recording are the bottleneck. Suggested ramp: **Weeks 1-2 daily as you planned; if retention (average view duration) drops, step down to 4-5 long/week + 3 Shorts/day.** Quality-per-video beats count for hitting 4,000 hours.
4. Watch hours realistic math: 4,000 h = 240,000 viewer-minutes ≈ 80,000 views at 3 min average. Longer (6-8 min) videos with strong retention reach it faster than many 3-minute ones, so consider making Episodes 2-6 about **5-7 minutes**.

## EPISODE 4 — Debt Snowball vs Debt Avalanche
**Title options:** "Debt Snowball vs Avalanche: Which Pays Off Faster?" • "Pay Off Debt Faster: Snowball or Avalanche?"
**Thumbnail:** snowball vs avalanche split, **"WHICH WINS?"**
**Hook:** "Two proven ways to crush debt. One saves more money, the other keeps you motivated. Let's run the numbers."
**Outline & visuals**
1. Hook: stack of debt cards with interest ticking up.
2. Define: **Snowball** = smallest balance first; **Avalanche** = highest interest rate first; both: pay minimums on everything else, roll freed-up payments forward.
3. Worked example (to be computed in the build script/spreadsheet, so figures are exact): 3 illustrative debts (e.g., $1,200 @ 24% APR, $3,500 @ 19%, $6,000 @ 7%), fixed extra $300/mo. Animated bar race: months to debt-free + total interest for each method. *Do not publish numbers until computed.*
4. How to choose: avalanche usually costs less interest; snowball gives faster early wins; pick the one you'll stick with.
5. Extra levers: balance-transfer/consolidation (mention risks, fees), call for lower APR, extra income. No personalized advice.
6. Where debt payoff fits the 50/30/20 (20% bucket) → CTA Ep.5.
**Shorts:** (1) "Snowball vs avalanche in 30 s" (2) "The extra $300 trick" (3) "Why minimum payments keep you stuck".
**Tags:** debt snowball, debt avalanche, pay off debt, credit card debt, debt free, personal finance
