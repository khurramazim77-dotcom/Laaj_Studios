# Narration script (record each line as its own file)

Save each take as `narration/<id>.wav` (or .mp3/.m4a), e.g. `narration/h1.wav`. Anything missing falls back to the AI voice.
Then run `python3 src/build.py audio && python3 src/build.py render` - the visuals re-time to your recording automatically.

## rule

- **r1**: Meet the 50/30/20 rule, popularized by Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi in the book All Your Worth.  _(spoken: Now meet the fifty, thirty, twenty rule, popularized by Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tee-ah-gee in the book, All Your Worth.)_
- **r2**: 50% of your take-home goes to needs.  _(spoken: Fifty percent of your take-home goes to needs.)_
- **r3**: 30% goes to wants.  _(spoken: Thirty percent goes to wants.)_
- **r4**: And 20% goes to savings and debt payoff.  _(spoken: And twenty percent goes to savings and debt payoff.)_
- **r5**: On $3,000, that's $1,500, $900, and $600.  _(spoken: On three thousand dollars, that's fifteen hundred, nine hundred, and six hundred.)_

## tips

- **p1**: Three tips to make this stick.
- **p2**: One: pay yourself first. Automate a transfer on payday, so your savings leave before you can spend them.
- **p3**: Two: track it in a simple spreadsheet. 10 minutes a week is all it takes.  _(spoken: Two: track it in a simple spreadsheet. Ten minutes a week is all it takes.)_
- **p4**: Three: adjust the rule to fit your life. If rent is high where you live, try 60/20/20. Progress beats perfection.  _(spoken: Three: adjust the rule to fit your life. If rent is high where you live, try sixty, twenty, twenty. Progress beats perfection.)_

## mistakes

- **m1**: Before we wrap up, three mistakes that quietly break budgets.
- **m2**: Mistake one: budgeting your gross salary instead of your take-home pay. That makes every number look bigger than it really is.
- **m3**: Mistake two: forgetting irregular costs, like car repairs, gifts, and annual fees. Set aside a little each month, so they don't surprise you.
- **m4**: Mistake three: making your budget so strict that you quit. Leave room for fun, because a budget you can stick with beats a perfect one you can't.
